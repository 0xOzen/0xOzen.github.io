[ CONTROL GATES · /06 ]

Six gates every adoption workflow has to answer.

When teams ask "is this AI safe to ship?", the productive answer isn't model talk - it's the same six questions, in order. Cards animate in as you scroll past.

1Owner Whose decision is this? Every workflow points to a named human accountable for the outcome.
2Permission What can it touch? Read, write, trigger and modify rights are scoped to the smallest viable surface.
3Approval Who signs off? Human checkpoints sit before commitments - money, identity, external messages.
4Evidence What is preserved? Inputs, prompts, outputs, decisions, reviewer notes - kept and queryable.
5Escalation Who gets paged? Each exception has a path - owner first, then on-call, then leadership.
6Recovery How do we undo? Reverse the action, revoke access, redo the work - rehearsed before scale.

From scattered AI pilots to one control plane.

Problem

Approach
Map workflows, name owners, set the suggest → commit autonomy ladder, attach evidence.
Outcome
A reviewable cockpit instead of invisible automation; safer to scale.
/ 02 Workflow design

From content volume to controlled workflow movement.

Problem
Teams measure AI by tokens written, not by work that actually moves.
Approach
Identify queues and handoffs, define approval gates, route exceptions to named humans.
Outcome
Cycle-time gains with audit-ready evidence and fewer stuck handoffs.
/ 03 Translation

From technical jargon to operator-grade language.

Problem
Leadership, legal and operations talk past each other when AI risk is on the table.
Approach
Replace abstract claims with concrete authority, evidence and recovery questions.
Outcome
Faster decisions, clearer ownership, fewer surprise escalations.
